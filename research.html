Research

Learning
to change.

I study learning systems under non-stationarity: how they retain useful knowledge, how they lose the ability to adapt, and how structure, representations, and behavioral diversity can preserve future learnability.

01

Continual learning · plasticity · non-stationarity

Future learnability

Continual learning is often framed as a retention problem: how can a system avoid forgetting what it already knows? I am equally interested in the other side of the problem — whether repeated learning changes the system in ways that make future learning progressively harder.

This leads to a broader question: can we evaluate and design learning systems not only for present performance, but also for the quality of the learning trajectories they leave available in the future?

02

Structural plasticity · optimization · neural networks

Structure as part of learning

Most neural networks are optimized while their architecture remains fixed. Structural plasticity instead treats the architecture itself as a changing object: units can be added, removed, or reorganized while learning is underway.

My work studies the optimization consequences of these edits, especially the asymmetry between growing and pruning networks and the mechanisms that determine whether newly introduced capacity actually becomes useful.

03

Continual RL · quality diversity · behavior spaces

Policy repertoires for adaptation

A single historically successful policy is not necessarily the best starting point for a future environment. I explore continual reinforcement learning systems that preserve multiple competent behaviors and organize them in learned behavior spaces.

This connects quality-diversity methods with continual learning: diversity is not only an end-state objective, but a resource that may make later adaptation, recovery, and transfer easier.

04

Reinforcement learning · open-endedness · adaptive intelligence

Agents in changing worlds

Many learning benchmarks assume that the world is stationary while the learner changes. Real agents participate in the environments they inhabit, encounter new regimes, and may alter the conditions under which future decisions are made.

I am interested in algorithms and evaluation settings that take this longer-horizon view seriously: learning as an ongoing process rather than a sequence of isolated optimization problems.

Working perspective

Performance is a snapshot. Adaptability is a trajectory.
