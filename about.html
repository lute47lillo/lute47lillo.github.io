I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Vermont, working in the Neurobotics Lab with Nick Cheney. My research centers on how learning systems can remain adaptable under continual change.

LL

Background

From reasoning systems to adaptive agents.

I came to my current research through several different corners of computer science. My earlier work included network science and knowledge representation, including a master's thesis on epistemic logic programming. During my PhD, my focus shifted toward continual learning, reinforcement learning, and the mechanisms that determine whether trained systems remain capable of adaptation.

I am particularly drawn to problems where the usual static assumptions stop being comfortable: when the data distribution changes, when the dynamics of an environment shift, when the architecture itself changes during learning, or when a system must choose what to preserve for an unknown future.

Methodologically, I tend to prefer mechanism-focused empirical work: controlled interventions, diagnostics that expose what changed inside the learning process, and evaluations that distinguish immediate performance from longer-term adaptability.
